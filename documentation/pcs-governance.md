# PCS: governance and operating rules

These are intended operating rules for the clarified PCS model. The prototype does not yet enforce all of them.

## Public participants

The public participant names are **Donors** and **Receivers**. Approved charity organisations can receive support under the same transparency requirements; their organisational designation does not create an eighth individual eligibility category.

Receivers require an interview and approval by a PCS representative. They cannot self-register for assistance: the PCS team creates the account after interview, verification and approval. Their profiles are always public and include their name, location, photographs, video and contribution history. Race and religion are not profile fields. The internal team assists receivers who need help with participation.

Donors choose a public or private profile. Public donor profiles show whom they supported. Receiver contribution histories still show contributors when a donor's own profile is private. Both participant types have PCS volunteer achievements. Donations to individuals, donations to charity organisations, help received, unpaid volunteering, paid services and project participation have distinct linked records.

## Monthly limits and justified exceptions

The standard monthly allocation is 10,000 PCS, with 1 PCS = 1 USDT as the valuation reference. Additional allocations require a request explaining the need, supporting evidence and a recorded approval. The review must connect the request to the approved receiver and its allocation history.

Allocation, accumulated PCS balance and actual external assistance are distinct records. The receiver obtains external money through a confirmed donor purchase, not merely from receiving an allocation.

## Earnings and directors

All participants' PCS-related earnings must be publicly disclosed through their profiles and relevant records. This rule concerns PCS activity, not unrelated personal finances. Receiver assistance, service earnings and expense reimbursements must be identified accurately rather than combined into one unexplained profit figure.

PCS does not profit from donations. Directors cannot profit from any donation directly or indirectly, including operating and external-project donations. A connected supplier, intermediary or nominee must not be used to bypass that prohibition. Public disclosure does not create an exception.

Approved non-director staff and providers may be paid for legitimate work from the appropriate separate funding. Each service record identifies the charity or project and location, worker, work performed, date or period, rate, hours or units, total amount paid and linked payment record. Relevant project costs, relationships and PCS-related earnings are disclosed. A person may volunteer on one occasion and receive a legitimate payment for different work; those records remain separate.

## Funding separation

| Purpose | Intended use | Required public accountability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct assistance | The intended individual receiver or approved charity organisation | Actual assistance received, relevant PCS movements and contribution histories |
| Internal operations | Hosting, security, maintenance, verification, approved staff and participation support | Operating donations, spending, staff payments and available balance |
| External operations | Defined physical aid projects and their approved delivery costs | Responsible profiles, budgets, suppliers, supporting records, delivery and related earnings |

Direct receiver assistance must not fund PCS running expenses. Internal operating support is donated separately by companies and individuals. External projects have their own identified purpose and records.

## Country inspectors

Each country can have **one inspector account only**. Its privileged actions must be recorded. The national account requires an accountable custodian and appropriately restricted access; hiding its public profile must not hide its actions from authorised review.

Inspector access supports authorised government and law-enforcement oversight. This is an intended capability, not a claim of an existing official partnership. Sensitive official access details and raw verification evidence require access controls.

Receiver assistance by the internal team and management review are separate responsibilities. A management countersignature for a sensitive change does not require another inspector account for that country.

## Partner SOPs

Existing charities, representatives, suppliers and other participants must accept the practices applicable to their work:

1. Follow the PCS workflow for authorised charitable activity and record the relevant payments.
2. Do not collect unrecorded cash or undisclosed side payments. PCS treats unauthorised collections as theft under its SOPs and investigates them.
3. Disclose relevant earnings, costs, supplier relationships and conflicts of interest.
4. Provide evidence of legitimate expenditure and project delivery.
5. Cooperate with authorised checks and inspection.
6. Protect vulnerable people from coercion, financial exploitation and sexual exploitation. Media and representation must respect consent and dignity.

PCS selects organisations willing to accept this standard. Breaches can lead to suspension, investigation and removal, with relevant evidence preserved.

## Records and review

The ledger records platform movements; verification connects those movements with real external payments and project activity. Reconciliation compares these records to identify discrepancies. A recorded assertion must not substitute for confirming the actual event.

Original transaction records remain available. Corrections are new linked transactions. Exceptional coin allocations, payment-destination changes and sensitive related-party decisions need reasons, evidence and recorded review. See [Simple controls](pcs-simple-controls.md) for a minimal implementation approach.
